[SECTION:cold_open]
At 11:47 at night, I watched eleven tiny “listened” icons appear under a voice note that was not supposed to leave my phone.

The note was one minute and fifty-two seconds long, and it had gone to the office group chat. My manager was in that group. So were both interns.

[SECTION:setup]
That morning, someone had announced a Saturday team-building day.

I was fine with the Saturday part. I was less fine with the phrase “high-energy icebreaker.”

So that night I recorded a voice note for my friend Sam.

I said I would rather file my taxes in a moving car than explain my spirit animal to Accounting.

The note was dramatic and private for about three seconds.

[SECTION:wrong_chat]
Sam's chat sat directly under a group called “Saturday Offsite.”

I tapped, held the button, delivered my tiny speech, and hit send without reading the name at the top.

Then Sam texted me separately.

“Wrong chat.”

I looked back at the screen and felt every useful thought leave my body at once.

[SECTION:damage_control]
I found “delete for everyone,” but eleven people had already listened.

So I typed an apology.

It started with “For context,” which is how you know an apology is about to create more context.

I deleted that too.

Then my manager reacted to the original voice note with a thumbs-up and wrote, “Looking forward to your energy tomorrow.”

[SECTION:next_morning]
The next morning, everyone was painfully kind to me.

Nobody mentioned the voice note. This was worse.

The organizer had replaced the icebreaker with “share one fun fact.”

When it reached me, my manager smiled and said, “You seem to have strong feelings about group activities. Why don't you start?”

My fun fact was that I had recently learned how long one minute and fifty-two seconds can feel.

[SECTION:button]
I renamed the office chat “DO NOT SEND AUDIO HERE.”

That solved the problem.

Mostly.

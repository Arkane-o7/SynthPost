# Research notes

## Core finding

Private credit is negotiated nonbank lending, typically to below-investment-grade or middle-market companies. It grew because borrowers value speed, certainty, confidentiality and customized terms, while long-duration institutional capital lets funds hold illiquid loans.

## Sourced facts used in the script

- The Federal Reserve's May 2026 Financial Stability Report estimates that, in the second half of 2025, private credit loans represented about **$1.4 trillion**, **10% of U.S. nonfinancial corporate debt**, and about **one-third of below-investment-grade debt excluding bank loans**.
- The same Fed report says banks continued lending to private-credit funds and BDCs; credit can migrate outside a bank's balance sheet without eliminating bank exposure to the financing chain.
- The report counts **$425 billion gross / $241 billion net** across perpetual-life BDCs and interval funds, about 20% of private-credit-vehicle net assets under management.
- The IMF's April 2024 Global Financial Stability Report says near-term systemic risks appeared contained, partly because most funds use long-term capital, while warning about opacity, floating-rate borrowers, weaker underwriting, subjective valuations and interconnected leverage.
- BIS Bulletin 106 notes that retail vehicles can bring useful price signals but can also reintroduce liquidity pressure into an illiquid asset class.

## Editorial caveat

The episode distinguishes total private-credit market estimates from narrower vehicle-level figures and does not imply that private credit is uniformly safer or riskier than bank lending.
